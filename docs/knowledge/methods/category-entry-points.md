# Category Entry Points meeting method

**Status:** working method for Ari's monthly client direction meeting.
**Primary source:** Prof. Jenni Romaniuk, *Category Entry Points in a Business-to-Business
(B2B) world*, July 2022.
**Built on:** C1, C7 and C8 in [the Ehrenberg-Bass claims register](../ehrenberg-bass-claims.md).

**The source paper is NOT held in this repository.** No PDF, no extracted text.
Everything below attributed to Romaniuk was written without access to it, so the
attributions are claims about the source, not checks against it. They must not be
quoted to a client until someone with the paper has verified them.

**Source discipline:** each step below is labelled **Sourced (verified)**,
**Attributed — UNVERIFIED**, or **Our operationalisation**. *Sourced (verified)*
means it was checked against C1/C7/C8 in this repo, which were extracted from PDFs
directly. *Attributed — UNVERIFIED* means it is presented as coming from the paper
but nobody has confirmed it. *Our operationalisation* is Ari's working adaptation for a
small-business, short-form-social meeting. The 60-minute format and the printable
worksheet are operational constraints from this project, not claims made by the paper.

**Standing caveat:** the evidence base is B2B, and much of it is long-purchase-cycle B2B.
Ari's clients are often small local businesses, and the delivery channel is short-form social.
Nothing in the source shows that the method transfers cleanly to that setting. Use this as a
disciplined way to choose situations to build memory around, not as proof that a reel, post or
carousel will cause sales.

---

## What a CEP is

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** A Category Entry Point is a cue that helps a buyer's memory retrieve possible
brands when a buying situation starts. Cues can be internal, like a motive or emotion, and
external, like a place, time, activity or person involved. The source paper's practical point is
that brands can become easier to think of by repeatedly linking the brand to those situations.

**Our operationalisation:** In a client meeting, do not start with "mental availability." Say:

> A CEP is the moment someone starts needing you. It is the situation in their life, work or day
> that makes your category relevant.

**Our operationalisation:** A good CEP answer names a situation. A weak answer names a product
feature, a demographic, a channel, or a brand slogan.

| Weak answer | Why it is weak | Better direction |
|---|---|---|
| "Busy parents" | A group, not a buying situation | "When school starts next week and the uniform still does not fit" |
| "Friendly service" | A brand claim, not a retrieval cue | "When they are anxious because the last provider made them feel stupid" |
| "Instagram" | A channel, not a need | "When they see a room/event/problem and realise they need help before the weekend" |
| "We are open late" | A feature | "When the problem appears after normal business hours" |

## Run the meeting

### Open the frame

**Our operationalisation:** Start by saying that the goal is not to invent campaign themes. The
goal is to find the situations the client should become easy to remember in.

Use this script:

> We are looking for the moments where a customer first thinks, "I need someone for this." If we
> can name those moments clearly, we can make content that keeps putting your business beside
> those moments.

### Elicit candidate situations

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** Romaniuk uses the W prompts to generate CEPs: when, where, while, with what, why,
how feeling, and with or for whom. In B2B, she also distinguishes the business need from the
decision-maker's professional or personal motive.

**Our operationalisation:** Ask the owner the questions below in order. The order is designed
for a live meeting: start with the recent concrete cases, then widen into motives, emotions and
context.

1. Think about recent good customers. What had just happened before they contacted you?
2. What were they trying to get done, fix, avoid or feel?
3. What words did they use before they knew what to ask for?
4. When does this need usually appear: time of day, week, season, deadline or life event?
5. Where are they when the need becomes obvious?
6. What are they doing before, during or after the need appears?
7. What else does your product or service have to work with?
8. Who else is affected by the choice, or has to approve it?
9. What makes the situation urgent enough that they stop ignoring it?
10. Which situations are customers embarrassed, annoyed, anxious or relieved about?

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** The paper says CEPs can come from category buyers, marketers and, where available,
sales teams. It also says the W prompts can be used in surveys or as brainstorming prompts.

**Our operationalisation:** For Ari's clients, the owner interview is a substitute for a proper
category-buyer survey when no survey exists. That makes the list provisional. Mark entries as
"heard from customers," "owner inference" or "Ari inference" so the source quality is visible.

### When the owner gets stuck

**Our operationalisation:** Owners will often answer with what they sell or who they sell to.
Convert the answer back into a situation instead of accepting it as a CEP.

| If they say... | Ask instead... |
|---|---|
| A feature | "When does that feature matter enough for someone to act?" |
| A demographic | "What is happening to that person when they need you?" |
| A value like trust, quality or speed | "What went wrong, or what are they afraid will go wrong, that makes that value important?" |
| A competitor comparison | "What situation makes them compare providers in the first place?" |
| Everyone | "Tell me about a real recent customer. What was happening before they reached out?" |
| Price | "What pressure makes price the deciding issue in that moment?" |

## Prioritise the list

### Use elimination, not scoring

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** Romaniuk frames prioritisation as elimination. Remove poor choices so the viable
opportunities remain. The paper is said to use three "C" forces: credibility,
competitiveness and commonality/currency.

> The third term is written here as "commonality/currency" because the writer was
> not certain which word the paper uses. That uncertainty is itself the reason this
> whole section is UNVERIFIED. **Check this one first.**

**Our operationalisation:** In this small-business meeting, do not create a made-up score. Ask
the following questions and keep only candidates that survive discussion. If a threshold would
be useful here, no source in this repo supports one for this setting.

| Filter | Source status | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Credibility | Attributed — UNVERIFIED | Can this business genuinely deliver for this situation now, and would customers believe that? |
| Competitiveness | Attributed — UNVERIFIED | Is this situation already a cluttered mental space where every competitor sounds the same? |
| Commonality/currency | Attributed — UNVERIFIED | Does this situation happen often enough, or create enough commercial value, to be worth building around? |
| Content fit | Our operationalisation | Can we show or explain this situation in short-form social without faking evidence or making the client sound generic? |
| Distinctive branding | Our operationalisation from C8 | Can the content make the client's brand recognisable while talking about the situation? |

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** The paper gives a research-survey method for external credibility, including a
free-choice brand-by-CEP table and a plus-or-minus five percentage point classification band for
mental advantage or disadvantage.

**Our operationalisation:** Do not use that five-point band in the meeting unless you have done
the survey. In a normal client direction meeting, write "survey not run" and treat credibility as
a judgement call.

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** The paper names a "long-short list" of five to eight CEPs as the eventual messaging
opportunities for a brand over time.

**Our operationalisation:** Do not force five to eight in the meeting. That number belongs to
the paper's B2B planning context after screening. For an owner-led small-business meeting, keep
a working list of the situations that survive the filters and say explicitly that it is provisional.

## Turn a CEP into content

### Keep the situation and the brand together

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** The paper says brand associations are built when the buyer experiences the brand
and the source of the association together. It also says each execution should focus on one CEP,
while the brand builds links to more CEPs over time.

**Our operationalisation:** Each post, reel, carousel or article should make one situation vivid,
then put the client's brand beside that situation. Use this structure:

1. Situation: "When this happens..."
2. Customer state: what they are trying to fix, avoid, achieve or feel.
3. Category bridge: why this is the moment the category becomes relevant.
4. Brand presence: a recognisable client cue, proof point, owner voice or distinctive asset.
5. Next action: what the viewer can do now, without pretending they are ready to buy.

**Sourced from C1:** Most people seeing the content may not be ready to buy now. The job is to
build a memory link that can activate later.

**Sourced from C7:** Growth depends on reaching more buyers, not only deepening loyalty among
current customers.

**Sourced from C8:** Small brands should expect future customers to have stronger memory links
to bigger competitors, so distinctive branding matters in ordinary content, not only in campaigns.

### Worked examples

**Our operationalisation:** These examples are invented small-business translations, not sourced
findings.

| Business | Candidate CEP | Content angle |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency dentist | Tooth pain starts after normal business hours | Reel opens on the moment: Friday night, pain is getting worse, and the viewer is deciding whether it can wait. The brand appears with the exact after-hours process. |
| Local accountant | The owner realises the books are not ready before a tax deadline | Carousel names the situation, shows what to gather first, and frames the accountant as the calm next step. |
| Hair salon | A customer notices regrowth before a specific event | Short post starts with the event pressure, then shows what can and cannot be fixed in time. |
| Physiotherapist | Pain starts interfering with sleep or work | Reel names the daily-life disruption before discussing treatment, so the cue is the situation rather than the service list. |

## Measure it over time

### What the source can measure

**Attributed — UNVERIFIED:** Romaniuk's measurement approach uses customer memory data. The three Mental
Availability metrics are Mental Market Share, Mental Penetration and Network Size. These require
a category-buyer survey where respondents can link brands to CEPs.

**Our operationalisation:** A one-operator setup usually cannot run that properly for every
small client. Without the survey, do not claim to have measured mental availability.

### What Ari can reasonably watch

**Our operationalisation:** Track weaker, directional signals that keep the method honest:

- Which CEP each published item was built around.
- Whether comments, DMs, calls or intake forms repeat the same customer language.
- Whether the owner hears customers naming the situation back.
- Whether enquiries mention the trigger situation rather than only the service.
- Whether a CEP keeps producing usable content without drifting into generic tips.

**Our operationalisation:** These signals can guide the next meeting, but they do not prove
causality, sales lift or category-wide mental availability. If a client needs that level of
evidence, the source-compatible move is a proper free-choice CEP survey.
